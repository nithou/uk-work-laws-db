# Preparing a claim

## 1. Evidence checklist

| Always | By claim type |
|---|---|
| Contract and written statement, handbook and policies, payslips (12+ months), P45, letters of offer/promotion, appraisals, emails and messages, notes of meetings (made at the time), timeline with dates, names of witnesses | **Unfair dismissal**: invitation letters, investigation report, witness statements, disciplinary/appeal outcome, comparators treated more leniently. **Discrimination**: comparators' treatment, statistics, remarks, medical evidence (disability impact statement), grievance and response. **Pay/holiday**: rotas, timesheets, contract pay terms, holiday records. **Whistleblowing**: copies of disclosures and dates, sequence of detriments. **Redundancy**: consultation documents, selection scores, alternative roles offered |

Covert recordings: tribunals may admit them if relevant; making one may be misconduct and affect remedy (Phoenix House v Stockman [2016]). Prefer contemporaneous notes and requesting minutes.

A **subject access request** (UK GDPR art. 15) obtains personal data, including emails about the employee, within one month (extendable by two months for complex requests).

## 2. Drafting the ET1 "details of claim"

1. Parties and employment: dates, job, pay, hours, status, length of service, size of employer.
2. Facts in chronological numbered paragraphs, with dates.
3. Legal claims, each identified: e.g. "unfair dismissal contrary to ss.94 and 98 ERA 1996", "discrimination arising from disability (s.15 EqA)", "failure to make reasonable adjustments (ss.20–21 EqA): the PCP was…; the disadvantage was…; the adjustments that should have been made were…", "unlawful deductions from wages (s.13 ERA)".
4. For discrimination, list each act complained of (date, who, what) and the protected characteristic; identify comparators (actual or hypothetical).
5. Remedy sought: compensation, reinstatement/re-engagement, declaration, recommendation, ACAS uplift.

## 4. Witness statement tips

First person, chronological, numbered paragraphs; reference bundle pages; stick to facts you witnessed; deal with the key disputes; explain impact (finances, health, job search — mitigation evidence: applications, interviews, new job offers). Bring a copy for each panel member if not shared electronically.

## 5. Mitigation

The claimant must take reasonable steps to find work; keep a job-search log. Accepting a lower-paid job can be reasonable; retraining or self-employment can be reasonable if sensible.

## 6. Settlement during proceedings

Acas conciliation remains available throughout (COT3). Without prejudice offers; "Calderbank" offers (without prejudice save as to costs) may affect costs. Judicial mediation for suitable cases.
