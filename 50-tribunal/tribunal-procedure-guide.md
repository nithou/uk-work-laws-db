# Employment tribunal procedure (England, Wales and Scotland)

The procedure is governed by the **Employment Tribunal Procedure Rules 2024** (in force 6 January 2025), with Presidential Practice Directions and Guidance. The **overriding objective** (rule 3) is to deal with cases fairly and justly: equal footing, proportionality, avoiding unnecessary formality, avoiding delay, saving expense.

## 1. Before the claim

1. Raise a **written grievance** (unless dismissed — then appeal), following the Acas Code: failure by the employee to raise a grievance can reduce compensation by up to 25% where the Code applies.
2. Notify **Acas early conciliation** (online at acas.org.uk or by phone) within the time limit. Conciliation lasts up to 12 weeks; the EC certificate (with a reference number) is required for the ET1. Conciliation is free, confidential, and can continue after a claim is made.
3. Calculate the deadline with the stop-the-clock rules (see `40-guides/time-limits.md`).

## 2. The claim (ET1)

- Submitted online (MyHMCTS for representatives / GOV.UK "make a claim") or by post to the central office. **No fee** (fees were declared unlawful in R (Unison) v Lord Chancellor [2017] UKSC 51; the previous government consulted on reintroduction — none in force).
- Must include: claimant and respondent details, **EC certificate number** for each respondent (or exemption), and the details of claim — say what happened, when, and which legal claims are made (unfair dismissal, s.15 disability discrimination, unlawful deductions…). Later amendment needs permission (Selkent principles / Vaughan v Modality [2021]).
- Multiple respondents: employer and individual harassers.
- The tribunal can reject a claim lacking minimum information or with a mismatched EC certificate (rules 12–14 of the 2024 Rules) — reconsideration of rejection is possible.

## 3. The response (ET3)

Respondent has **28 days** from the date the claim was sent to submit the ET3 (rules 16–17). Without a response, a judgment can be issued (rule 22). Employer's contract claim (counterclaim) possible in response to an employee's contract claim.

## 4. Case management

- **Preliminary hearing (case management)**, often by video: list of issues, directions (disclosure, bundle, witness statements, schedule of loss), hearing length and date. Judicial mediation may be offered in some cases.
- **Preliminary hearing (public)**: to decide time limits, employment status, disability status, strike-out or **deposit orders** (up to £1,000 per allegation with little reasonable prospect of success).
- **Disclosure**: each party lists and copies relevant documents, including those adverse to its case; requests for specific disclosure; **subject access requests** under UK GDPR are a parallel route.
- **Schedule of loss**: itemised calculation of compensation claimed, updated before the hearing.
- **Witness statements**: full evidence in chief, numbered paragraphs, page references to the bundle; usually exchanged simultaneously.
- **Bundle**: agreed, paginated; respondent usually prepares it.

## 5. Final hearing

- Unfair dismissal claims usually heard by an Employment Judge alone; discrimination and whistleblowing claims often by a judge with two non-legal members.
- Order: party with the burden goes first (respondent first in unfair dismissal where dismissal is admitted).
- Each witness confirms the statement, is cross-examined, may be re-examined; tribunal questions; closing submissions.
- **Judgment**: oral at the end or reserved and sent in writing. Written reasons must be requested within 14 days of an oral judgment (rule 60) — essential for appeal.
- **Remedy** hearing may follow liability.

## 6. Costs (rules 72–82)

Costs are the **exception**: an order may be made where a party (or representative) acted vexatiously, abusively, disruptively or otherwise unreasonably, or the claim or response had no reasonable prospect of success, or a hearing was postponed on late application. The tribunal may consider ability to pay. Up to £20,000 without detailed assessment. Preparation time orders for unrepresented parties.

## 7. After the judgment

- **Reconsideration** by the tribunal: application within 14 days of the written judgment being sent, where necessary in the interests of justice (rules 68–71).
- **Appeal to the Employment Appeal Tribunal** (EAT) on a **point of law** only: within **42 days** of the date the written reasons were sent (EAT Rules). Free. Grounds must show an error of law or a perverse decision.
- Further appeal: Court of Appeal (England and Wales) / Court of Session (Scotland), then Supreme Court, with permission.
- **Enforcement**: unpaid awards — county court (E&W, via the Employment Tribunal Fast Track or High Court enforcement officers) or sheriff officers (Scotland); Insolvency Service if the employer is insolvent; financial penalties on employers for unpaid awards (Employment Tribunals Act s.37E–37Q).

## 8. Scotland

Employment Tribunals (Scotland) apply the same Rules. Differences: Glasgow, Edinburgh, Aberdeen and Dundee offices; enforcement by extract and sheriff officers; Scottish case law (Inner House) binding on Scottish tribunals; appeals from the EAT go to the Court of Session; interest rates and some remedies differ.

## 9. Free and low-cost support

Trade unions (often include legal representation), Acas helpline, Citizens Advice, law centres, Free Representation Unit (London and South-East), Advocate (pro bono barristers), university law clinics, legal expenses insurance attached to home or car insurance, conditional fee / damages-based agreements with solicitors.
