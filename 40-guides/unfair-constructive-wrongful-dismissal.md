# Dismissal: unfair, automatically unfair, constructive and wrongful

## 1. Who can claim ordinary unfair dismissal

- An **employee** (not a worker or contractor) — ERA s.94.
- With the **qualifying period** of continuous employment ending with the EDT (s.108):
  - **2 years** where the EDT is before 1 January 2027;
  - **6 months** where the EDT is on or after 1 January 2027 (ERA 2025 s.25; SI 2026/559). The EDT is extended by the statutory minimum notice if the employer gave less (s.97(2)): an employee dismissed summarily a week short of the qualifying date may still qualify.
- No qualifying period for **automatically unfair** reasons (see 4).

## 2. Was there a dismissal? (s.95)

1. Termination by the employer, with or without notice.
2. Expiry of a fixed-term contract without renewal.
3. **Constructive dismissal**: the employee resigns because of a **repudiatory breach** of contract by the employer (Western Excavating v Sharp [1978]). Most often a breach of the implied term of **trust and confidence** — conduct calculated or likely to destroy or seriously damage the relationship without reasonable and proper cause (Malik v BCCI [1997] UKHL 23). A "last straw" can complete a course of conduct (Kaur v Leeds Teaching Hospitals [2018] EWCA Civ 978). The employee must resign in response to the breach and not delay too long (otherwise affirmation). A constructive dismissal is not automatically unfair: the tribunal then asks whether it was fair, which rarely succeeds for employers.

Resignation letters should state that the employee is resigning in response to the employer's breach, and list the main reasons.

## 3. Ordinary unfair dismissal: the two-stage test (s.98)

**Stage 1 — reason.** The employer must show the reason (or principal reason) and that it is a potentially fair one:
- capability or qualifications (performance, ill health);
- conduct;
- redundancy;
- statutory illegality (continuing would breach a statutory duty — e.g. loss of right to work, driving ban for a driver);
- **some other substantial reason** (SOSR): e.g. business reorganisation, breakdown of working relationship, third-party pressure, expiry of a fixed-term contract, refusal of a contract change ("fire and rehire").

**Stage 2 — reasonableness (s.98(4)).** Did the employer act reasonably in treating that reason as sufficient, in the circumstances (including size and administrative resources), in accordance with equity and the substantial merits? The tribunal must not substitute its own view: the question is whether dismissal fell within the **band of reasonable responses** (Iceland Frozen Foods v Jones [1983]; Post Office v Foley [2000]), which applies to the investigation as well as the sanction (Sainsbury's v Hitt [2002] EWCA Civ 1588).

### Misconduct — the Burchell test (BHS v Burchell [1978])

1. Did the employer genuinely believe the employee was guilty?
2. Did it have reasonable grounds for that belief?
3. Had it carried out as much investigation as was reasonable?

Plus a fair procedure (Acas Code) and a sanction within the band. Gross misconduct allows summary dismissal under the contract, but the dismissal can still be unfair.

### Capability — performance

Clear standards, warnings, support or training, a reasonable time to improve, and consideration of alternatives.

### Capability — ill health

Genuine consultation with the employee, up-to-date medical evidence (occupational health), consideration of adjustments and alternative work, and whether the employer can reasonably be expected to wait any longer (East Lindsey DC v Daubney [1977]; BS v Dundee City Council [2013] CSIH 91). Check disability discrimination at the same time.

### Procedure — the Acas Code of Practice on disciplinary and grievance procedures

Establish the facts (investigation); inform the employee in writing of the problem with enough information (including evidence) to prepare; hold a meeting; allow the **right to be accompanied** by a colleague or trade union representative (Employment Relations Act 1999 s.10 — the request must be reasonable; the hearing may be postponed up to 5 working days if the companion is unavailable); decide on appropriate action and inform in writing; provide a right of **appeal**. A **procedurally unfair** dismissal is unfair even if the outcome would have been the same, but compensation may then be reduced (Polkey v AE Dayton Services [1987] UKHL 8).

## 4. Automatically unfair reasons (no qualifying period in most cases)

| Reason | Section |
|---|---|
| Pregnancy, maternity, paternity, adoption, parental, shared parental, neonatal care, carer's leave, time off for dependants | ERA s.99 and family leave regulations |
| Health and safety activities, raising H&S concerns, leaving a dangerous workplace | s.100 |
| Refusing Sunday work (shop/betting workers) | s.101 |
| Working time rights (refusal to breach WTR, asserting rights) | s.101A |
| Acting as an employee representative or candidate | s.103 |
| **Protected disclosure (whistleblowing)** | s.103A |
| Asserting a statutory right (in good faith) | s.104 |
| Flexible working request | s.104C |
| National minimum wage | s.104A |
| Trade union membership or activities, use of union services | TULRCA s.152 |
| Taking part in protected industrial action (within the protected period and protections strengthened from 18 February 2026) | TULRCA s.238A |
| TUPE transfer as the sole or principal reason | TUPE reg. 7 |
| Spent convictions, jury service, pension scheme membership, part-time or fixed-term status, blacklisting, etc. | various |

Discrimination dismissals are pursued under the Equality Act (no qualifying period, uncapped compensation).

## 5. Remedies for unfair dismissal

1. **Reinstatement** or **re-engagement** (s.113–117) — rarely ordered, must be asked for.
2. **Basic award** (s.119): ½ week's pay per complete year of service under 22, 1 week's pay aged 22–40, 1½ weeks' pay aged 41+, maximum 20 years, week's pay capped at **£751** (from 6 April 2026) → maximum **£22,530**. Minimum basic award of **£9,157** for certain automatically unfair reasons (H&S, employee representatives, whistleblowing is not included — see s.120; trade union dismissals TULRCA s.156).
3. **Compensatory award** (s.123): what is just and equitable for the loss suffered — past and future loss of earnings and benefits (including pension), loss of statutory rights (a conventional sum), expenses; subject to the duty to **mitigate**.
   - **Cap**: the lower of **£123,543** or **52 weeks' gross pay** (s.124, for EDTs from 6 April 2026). No cap for whistleblowing or H&S dismissals (s.124(1A)).
   - **The cap is abolished** for dismissals with an EDT on or after **1 January 2027** (ERA 2025 s.25(3) omits s.124).
4. **Adjustments**:
   - **Polkey** reduction: percentage chance the employee would have been fairly dismissed anyway.
   - **Contributory fault** (s.122(2), s.123(6)): blameworthy conduct contributing to the dismissal.
   - **Acas uplift / reduction**: up to **25%** for unreasonable failure to comply with the Acas Code (TULRCA s.207A) — increase if the employer failed, decrease if the employee failed (e.g. no appeal).
   - Failure to give a written statement of particulars: 2 or 4 weeks' pay (Employment Act 2002 s.38), capped week's pay.
   - Order of adjustments matters (ERA s.124A: the Acas adjustment and the Employment Act 2002 s.38 award are applied immediately before contributory-fault reductions; the statutory cap, while it applies, is applied last). Check the case law before computing a final figure.
5. **Recoupment**: certain state benefits received are recouped from the award.

## 6. Wrongful dismissal (breach of contract)

Dismissal without the notice required by the contract or by statute, when the employee did not commit a repudiatory breach (gross misconduct). No qualifying period. Damages = net pay and benefits for the notice period (subject to mitigation unless payment in lieu is contractually due). Tribunal limit for breach-of-contract claims: **£25,000**; no limit in the county court / High Court (England and Wales) or sheriff court / Court of Session (Scotland), where the limitation period is 6 years (E&W) or 5 years prescription (Scotland).

**Statutory minimum notice** from the employer (s.86): 1 week after 1 month's service; 1 week per complete year from 2 years; maximum 12 weeks. From the employee: 1 week (unless the contract says more).

## 7. Written reasons and evidence

- Request **written reasons for dismissal** (s.92): must be given within 14 days; entitlement after 2 years' service, or at any time if dismissed while pregnant or on maternity/adoption leave. Unreasonable failure: 2 weeks' pay.
- **Subject access request** under UK GDPR (Article 15) for personal data: one month to respond (extendable).
- Keep notes of meetings, copies of policies, emails; ask for the investigation report and witness statements.

## 8. Pre-termination negotiations ("protected conversations")

Offers and discussions with a view to ending employment on agreed terms are **inadmissible** in an ordinary unfair dismissal claim (ERA s.111A), unless there is improper behaviour (e.g. undue pressure, harassment) or the claim is automatically unfair. They remain admissible in discrimination and whistleblowing claims. Separate from the common-law "without prejudice" rule, which requires an existing dispute. See `settlement-agreements.md`.
