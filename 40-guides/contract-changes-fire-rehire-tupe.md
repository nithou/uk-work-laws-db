# Changes to terms, "fire and rehire" and TUPE

## 1. Changing a contract

An employer can change terms only:
1. with the employee's **agreement** (express, or implied by working under new terms without protest for a significant period — protest in writing and "work under protest");
2. under a **clear contractual right** to vary (interpreted narrowly; cannot be used unreasonably or to make fundamental changes — Wandsworth LBC v D'Silva [1998]); 
3. through a **collective agreement** incorporated into contracts;
4. by terminating the contract with notice and offering a new one ("dismissal and re-engagement").

Imposing a change without agreement or right is a breach of contract. Options for the employee: work under protest and claim (deductions or breach of contract for pay cuts); resign and claim constructive dismissal if the breach is repudiatory; or, for a fundamental change, treat the new terms as a dismissal from the old contract (Hogg v Dover College [1990]).

Written notice of changes to particulars within 1 month (ERA s.4).

## 2. Dismissal and re-engagement ("fire and rehire")

- **Statutory Code of Practice on Dismissal and Re-engagement** (in force 18 July 2024): employers must explore alternatives, consult genuinely and for as long as reasonably possible, share information, and use dismissal and re-engagement only as a last resort. Unreasonable failure to follow the Code: tribunal can adjust awards by up to **25%**.
- Collective consultation duties apply if 20 or more employees at one establishment would be dismissed within 90 days (dismissal for not accepting new terms is a dismissal "for redundancy" for consultation purposes: TULRCA s.195).
- Current law (until the ERA 2025 change): dismissal for refusing new terms is potentially fair as SOSR if the employer acted reasonably (sound business reason; reasonable process; Garside & Laycock v Booth [2011]).
- **ERA 2025 s.28** (expected **March 2027**): dismissal because the employee refused a variation of certain key terms (pay, hours, holiday, pensions, etc.) — or to replace them with someone doing the same role on varied terms — becomes **automatically unfair**, unless the employer shows it faced financial difficulties threatening its ability to carry on the business, could not reasonably avoid the need for the variation, and followed a fair process. Until then, rely on the current law and the Code.

## 3. TUPE — Transfer of Undertakings (Protection of Employment) Regulations 2006

**When it applies (reg. 3)**: transfer of an undertaking or business (or part) retaining its identity, or a **service provision change** (outsourcing, re-tendering to a new contractor, insourcing) where there is an organised grouping of employees principally dedicated to the client's activities (not one-off tasks or mainly goods supply).

**Effects**:
| Rule | Regulation |
|---|---|
| Employment of assigned employees transfers automatically on existing terms with continuity (employee may object, which ends employment without dismissal) | reg. 4 |
| Changes to terms are **void** if the sole or principal reason is the transfer, unless an economic, technical or organisational (ETO) reason entailing changes in the workforce, or permitted by the contract; terms deriving from a collective agreement can be varied after 1 year if overall no less favourable (reg. 4A) | regs. 4(4)–(5), 4A |
| Dismissal because of the transfer is **automatically unfair** unless for an ETO reason entailing changes in the workforce (then potentially fair as redundancy or SOSR) | reg. 7 |
| Substantial change in working conditions to the material detriment of the employee: can resign and be treated as dismissed | reg. 4(9) |
| Transferor must give employee liability information to the transferee at least **28 days** before the transfer | reg. 11 |
| **Information and consultation** of appropriate representatives (recognised union or elected employee reps; where there are no existing representatives, employers with fewer than 50 employees, or any employer transferring fewer than 10 employees, may inform and consult the employees directly — reg. 13A, for transfers on or after 1 July 2024) "long enough before" the transfer about its date, reasons, implications and measures envisaged | regs. 13–13A |
| Remedy for failure to inform or consult: up to **13 weeks' pay** per affected employee (no cap on week's pay), joint and several liability of transferor and transferee | regs. 15–16 |
| Pensions: occupational pension rights do not transfer (except some early retirement benefits) but minimum pension protection applies (Pensions Act 2004 s.257–258) | reg. 10 |
| Insolvency: modified protections | regs. 8–9 |

Representatives' rights: time off, facilities, protection (TUPE regs. 13–14; ERA ss.47, 61, 103).
