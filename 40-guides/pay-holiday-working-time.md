# Pay, holiday and working time

## 1. Written statement and payslips

- Every **worker** (not only employees) is entitled to a written statement of particulars **on or before day one** (ERA s.1): pay, hours, holiday, sick pay, notice, probation, training, pensions, disciplinary rules (can be in a reference document), etc. Changes notified in writing within one month (s.4). Tribunal can award 2 or 4 weeks' pay when another claim succeeds (Employment Act 2002 s.38).
- **Itemised pay statement** for every worker (s.8), showing gross pay, deductions and, where pay varies with time worked, the number of hours paid.

## 2. Unlawful deductions from wages (ERA Part II)

A deduction is unlawful unless required by statute (tax, NI), authorised by a written contractual term given to the worker beforehand, or agreed in writing **before** the conduct or event giving rise to it (s.13). Non-payment of wages properly payable (including holiday pay, commission, overtime due under the contract, statutory sick pay is enforced differently — via HMRC) counts as a deduction. Retail workers: cash shortages limited to 10% of gross pay per pay day (s.18). Tribunal claim within 6 months of the deduction or the last in a series (relevant dates from 1 October 2026); claims cannot go back more than **2 years** before the claim (s.23(4A)), except some types.

## 3. National minimum wage (from 1 April 2026)

| Category | Hourly rate |
|---|---|
| National living wage (21 and over) | **£12.71** |
| 18 to 20 | £10.85 |
| Under 18 | £8.00 |
| Apprentice (under 19, or first year of apprenticeship) | £8.00 |
| Accommodation offset | £11.10 per day |

(NMW Regulations 2015 regs. 4–4A, 16.) Pay is averaged over the **pay reference period**. Unpaid working time (training, security checks, travel between assignments, sleep-in shifts only when actually working — Royal Mencap v Tomlinson-Blake [2021] UKSC 8), deductions for uniforms or tools that take pay below the minimum, and salary sacrifice can all cause underpayment. Enforcement: HMRC NMW enforcement (now within the **Fair Work Agency**, established 7 April 2026), complaint free and confidential, arrears at current rates; tribunal claims as unlawful deductions or breach of contract; detriment and dismissal protection (ERA ss.23, 104A).

## 4. Tips (ERA Part 2B, in force since 1 October 2024)

All qualifying tips, gratuities and service charges must be passed to workers in full, distributed fairly (having regard to the statutory Code of Practice on fair and transparent distribution of tips), and paid by the end of the following month. Written tipping policy and records (3 years); workers can request records. Compensation up to £5,366 for failure to have a policy or records (from 6 April 2026). ERA 2025 s.14 adds consultation with workers and union representatives and a 3-yearly review — expected January 2027.

## 5. Holiday (Working Time Regulations 1998)

### Entitlement

- **5.6 weeks** a year (28 days for a five-day week; bank holidays may be included): 4 weeks under reg. 13 (EU-derived) + 1.6 weeks under reg. 13A. Workers as well as employees; from day one.
- **Irregular-hours and part-year workers** (leave years from 1 April 2024): accrue at **12.07% of hours worked** in each pay period (reg. 15B), capped at 28 days; **rolled-up holiday pay** (12.07% added to each payment, shown separately on the payslip) is permitted for them (reg. 16A); records of leave and pay (reg. 16B).
- Employers must keep adequate **holiday records** (duty in force since 6 April 2026, ERA 2025 s.35; failure is a criminal offence). Holiday enforcement by the Fair Work Agency expected April 2027.

### Holiday pay

- For the 4 weeks under reg. 13: **normal remuneration** — including regularly paid overtime, commission, payments linked to professional or personal status, and other regular payments (reg. 16 as amended from 1 January 2024, codifying Bear Scotland, Lock v British Gas, Chief Constable of the PSNI v Agnew [2023] UKSC 33).
- For the extra 1.6 weeks: basic pay is enough (unless the contract says otherwise).
- Variable pay: average over the previous **52 paid weeks** (reg. 16(3)).

### Carry-over and termination

- Reg. 13 leave can be carried over when the worker could not take it because of **sickness** (up to 18 months after the end of the leave year), **family-related leave**, or because the employer refused or failed to give the opportunity and encourage it (reg. 13(10)–(16), codifying King v Sash Window Workshop [2017] CJEU).
- On termination: payment in lieu for accrued untaken leave (reg. 14). The employer can require notice-period holiday to be taken if it gives the right notice (reg. 15).

### Claims

Tribunal claim under WTR reg. 30 (refusal or failure to pay) or as unlawful deductions (series of deductions, 2-year backstop). A gap of 3 months or more between underpayments may break the series (Agnew: not in Northern Ireland law; in GB the Bear Scotland 3-month gap rule remains the EAT position — check current authority).

## 6. Working time limits

| Rule | Limit | Regulation |
|---|---|---|
| Average weekly working time | **48 hours**, averaged over 17 weeks (26 or 52 in some cases) | reg. 4 — individual written **opt-out** possible (terminable on 7 days to 3 months' notice); no detriment for refusing |
| Daily rest | 11 consecutive hours per 24 | reg. 10 |
| Weekly rest | 24 hours per 7 days, or 48 hours per 14 days | reg. 11 |
| Rest break | 20 minutes if working more than 6 hours | reg. 12 |
| Night workers | Average 8 hours per 24; free health assessment | regs. 6, 7 |
| Young workers (under 18) | 8 hours a day, 40 a week; 12 hours' daily rest; 30-minute break after 4.5 hours | regs. 5A, 10(2), 12(4) |

Employers must take **all reasonable steps** to ensure the 48-hour limit is respected unless an opt-out exists, and keep records. Exceptions for autonomous workers whose working time is not measured or predetermined (reg. 20), and for specific sectors. Enforcement: HSE / local authorities for limits; tribunals for rest, breaks and holiday.

**Travel time**: for workers without a fixed place of work, travel from home to the first and from the last customer counts as working time (Tyco, CJEU 2015) — relevant to NMW only in specified situations.

## 7. Zero-hours and low-hours workers

- Exclusivity clauses are unenforceable for zero-hours contracts and low-income workers (ERA s.27A–27B).
- ERA 2025 (not yet in force — date to be confirmed after consultation): right to be offered guaranteed hours reflecting hours regularly worked over a reference period, reasonable notice of shifts, and payment for shifts cancelled or curtailed at short notice; extension to agency workers.
- The statutory right to request a more predictable working pattern (Workers (Predictable Terms and Conditions) Act 2023) has been repealed before commencement and replaced by these measures.
