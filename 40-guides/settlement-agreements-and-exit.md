# Settlement agreements and leaving employment

## 1. Settlement agreements

A settlement agreement is the only way (with an Acas **COT3**) to waive statutory employment claims validly (ERA s.203; EqA s.147). Conditions (s.203(3)):
- in writing;
- relates to the **particular complaints** or proceedings (a general "all claims" waiver is acceptable only if the claims are identified);
- the employee received **advice from a relevant independent adviser** (qualified lawyer, certified trade union official, certified advice centre worker, Fellow of ILEX) on its terms and effect on the ability to bring a tribunal claim;
- the adviser has **professional indemnity insurance**;
- the agreement **identifies the adviser** and states the conditions are satisfied.

Employers commonly contribute to legal fees (often £350–£750 + VAT).

**Typical terms to check**: termination date; payment in lieu of notice (taxable as "post-employment notice pay"); ex gratia sum; holiday pay; bonus/commission; shares; pension contributions; agreed **reference**; announcement wording; confidentiality and non-derogatory clauses (cannot stop protected disclosures — ERA s.43J — or disclosure to police, regulators, legal advisers and family; ERA 2025 s.24 will void NDAs covering harassment and discrimination, expected September 2027); return of property; post-termination restrictions; carve-outs (personal injury, accrued pension, enforcement of the agreement).

**Tax**: genuine compensation for loss of employment is tax-free up to **£30,000** (Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 ss.401–404); post-employment notice pay and contractual payments are taxed as earnings; employer Class 1A NICs on the excess over £30,000.

## 2. Protected conversations (ERA s.111A)

Pre-termination negotiations are inadmissible in an **ordinary unfair dismissal** claim unless improper behaviour (bullying, undue pressure, discrimination, putting no reasonable time to consider — Acas Code suggests at least 10 calendar days). They are **not** protected in discrimination, whistleblowing or automatically unfair dismissal claims. The common-law "without prejudice" rule protects genuine settlement attempts once a dispute exists.

## 3. Acas COT3

When Acas conciliates (before or after a claim), the agreed settlement is recorded on a **COT3** form, binding without the need for independent legal advice.

## 4. References

No general duty to give a reference (except in regulated sectors, e.g. FCA regulatory references); if given, it must be true, accurate and fair and not misleading (Spring v Guardian Assurance [1994] UKHL 7). A negative reference given because of a protected act can be victimisation.

## 5. Post-termination restrictions

Non-compete, non-solicitation and non-dealing covenants are enforceable only if they protect a legitimate business interest and go no further than reasonably necessary. Wrongful dismissal by the employer generally releases the employee from restrictions (General Billposting v Atkinson [1909]). The previous government's proposal to cap non-competes at 3 months was not legislated.

## 6. Final pay checklist

Last salary, notice pay or PILON, accrued holiday (WTR reg. 14), commission/bonus earned, expenses, statutory redundancy pay if applicable, P45, pension; check deductions (training cost clawbacks must be contractual and lawful).
