# Family leave and flexible working

Statutory family pay rates from April 2026: **£194.32 a week** or 90% of average weekly earnings if lower (SMP after the first 6 weeks; SPP, SAP, ShPP, statutory neonatal care pay, statutory parental bereavement pay). Lower earnings limit for statutory pay: £129 a week. Contracts often give more — check.

## 1. Pregnancy and maternity

| Right | Details |
|---|---|
| Paid time off for **antenatal care** (ERA s.55) | Day one; reasonable time off with pay. Partner: unpaid time off for up to 2 appointments (s.57ZE) |
| **Maternity leave** (ERA s.71–73; MPL Regs) | Day one: 26 weeks ordinary + 26 weeks additional = 52 weeks. Compulsory 2 weeks after birth (4 for factory workers). Notify by the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth |
| **SMP** | 26 weeks' service by the 15th week before EWC + earnings ≥ LEL: 6 weeks at 90% of AWE, then 33 weeks at £194.32 (or 90% if lower). Otherwise Maternity Allowance from DWP |
| Health and safety | Risk assessment; suspension on full pay if risks cannot be avoided (ERA s.66–68) |
| Protection | Pregnancy and maternity discrimination (EqA s.18), automatically unfair dismissal (ERA s.99), detriment protection; redundancy priority for suitable alternative vacancies during pregnancy, maternity leave and for **18 months** after birth (since 6 April 2024). Stronger protection against dismissal during pregnancy and after return is planned (ERA 2025 s.26 — expected October 2027) |
| Return | Right to return to the same job after ordinary leave; same or suitable job after additional leave (MPL Regs reg. 18) |

## 2. Paternity leave and pay

- Leave: 1 or 2 weeks, taken as one block of two weeks or two separate weeks, within **52 weeks** of birth or placement; notice 28 days. **Day one** right since 6 April 2026 (ERA 2025 ss.15–17); can now also be taken after shared parental leave.
- SPP: requires 26 weeks' service by the 15th week before EWC and earnings ≥ LEL.
- Bereaved partners: extended paternity leave (up to 52 weeks) if the mother or primary adopter dies (Paternity Leave (Bereavement) Act 2024, in force 29 December 2025; detailed rules in the Paternity and Adoption Leave Regulations as amended).

## 3. Adoption leave and pay

52 weeks' leave from day one for the primary adopter; SAP on the same pattern as SMP (eligibility 26 weeks' service); paid time off for pre-adoption appointments.

## 4. Shared parental leave (SPL)

Parents can share up to **50 weeks' leave** and **37 weeks' pay** (ShPP) by curtailing maternity or adoption leave. Eligibility: continuity test (26 weeks by the 15th week before EWC) for the employee, employment-and-earnings test for the partner. Up to 3 notices of leave; employer can refuse discontinuous blocks but not a continuous block. 20 SPLIT days (shared parental leave in touch days).

## 5. Unpaid parental leave

18 weeks per child up to the child's 18th birthday; maximum 4 weeks a year per child (default scheme); 21 days' notice; employer can postpone up to 6 months for business reasons (not at birth/adoption). **Day one** from 6 April 2026 (qualifying period of one year removed).

## 6. Neonatal care leave and pay (since 6 April 2025)

Up to **12 weeks** of leave for parents of a baby admitted to neonatal care within 28 days of birth for 7 continuous days or more; day-one leave; pay requires 26 weeks' service and earnings ≥ LEL. Taken within 68 weeks of birth.

## 7. Parental bereavement leave and pay

2 weeks (one block or two separate weeks) for the death of a child under 18 or a stillbirth after 24 weeks; taken within 56 weeks; day-one leave; pay requires 26 weeks' service. **General bereavement leave** (including pregnancy loss before 24 weeks) of up to two weeks unpaid is created by ERA 2025 s.18 — expected **April 2027**.

## 8. Carer's leave (since 6 April 2024)

One week of unpaid leave per rolling 12 months to provide or arrange care for a dependant with a long-term care need; can be taken in half days or full days; notice: twice the length of leave or 3 days if longer; employer can postpone but not refuse; day one.

## 9. Time off for dependants

Reasonable unpaid time off for emergencies (ERA s.57A), see `sickness-ssp-capability.md`.

## 10. Flexible working (ERA Part 8A; Flexible Working Regs 2014)

| Rule (since 6 April 2024) | Details |
|---|---|
| Eligibility | Employees from **day one** |
| Number of requests | 2 in any 12-month period |
| Content | Request in writing, stating the change; no longer need to explain the effect on the employer |
| Employer's duties | **Consult** the employee before refusing; decide within **2 months** (including any appeal) unless extended by agreement; deal with it in a reasonable manner; refuse only on one of the **8 business grounds** (s.80G(1)(b)): extra costs, ability to meet customer demand, reorganise work among staff, recruit staff, detrimental impact on quality, on performance, insufficient work during the proposed periods, planned structural changes |
| Remedy | Tribunal: up to 8 weeks' pay (capped week's pay) for procedural failures (s.80H–80I); plus possible indirect sex discrimination or disability claims, which are often stronger |
| Acas Code | Acas Code of Practice on flexible working requests (2024) |
| **ERA 2025 s.9** | Refusal will have to be **reasonable** on the specified ground, with the employer explaining why; expected **October 2027** (see the "⟦partly in force / prospective⟧" markers in s.80G of ERA 1996) |

## 11. Detriment and dismissal protection

All these rights carry protection against detriment and automatically unfair dismissal (ERA ss.47C, 99; family leave regulations), from day one.
