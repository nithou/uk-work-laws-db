# Redundancy

## 1. Definition (ERA s.139)

Dismissal wholly or mainly attributable to:
- the employer ceasing (or intending to cease) the business, or the business **in the place** where the employee was employed; or
- the requirements of the business for employees to carry out **work of a particular kind** (or in that place) having **ceased or diminished** (or being expected to).

The question is whether the need for employees to do work of a particular kind has diminished, not whether the work itself has (Murray v Foyle Meats [1999] UKHL 30). A reorganisation without a reduced need for employees is usually SOSR, not redundancy. Place of work: where the employee actually worked, not wherever a mobility clause could send them (High Table v Horst [1997]).

## 2. A fair redundancy dismissal (ordinary unfair dismissal rules)

Tribunals look for (Williams v Compair Maxam [1982]; Polkey [1987]):
1. **Warning and consultation** with affected employees (and representatives), at a formative stage, genuinely considering their responses.
2. A fair **pool** for selection (the range of reasonable responses: Capita Hartshead v Byard [2012]).
3. Fair, objective and consistently applied **selection criteria** (not tainted by discrimination, union membership, family leave, part-time status, whistleblowing). Last in, first out alone may be indirectly age-discriminatory.
4. Consideration of **suitable alternative employment** within the organisation and group.
5. A right of appeal (good practice; the Acas Code does not formally apply to redundancy, but tribunals expect fairness).

Selection for a reason that is automatically unfair (e.g. pregnancy, whistleblowing, trade union) makes the dismissal automatically unfair (ERA s.105).

**Pregnancy and family leave protection**: an employee on maternity, adoption or shared parental leave — and, since 6 April 2024, during pregnancy and for 18 months after birth or placement — has priority for any suitable available vacancy before redundancy (ERA s.49D and Part 5B; Maternity and Parental Leave Regs reg. 10). Breach makes the dismissal automatically unfair.

## 3. Rights of the redundant employee

| Right | Details |
|---|---|
| **Statutory redundancy pay** (ss.135, 162) | 2 years' continuous employment. ½ week's pay per complete year under 22; 1 week aged 22–40; 1½ weeks aged 41+; up to 20 years (counting backwards from the relevant date); week's pay capped at **£751** (from 6 April 2026). Maximum **£22,530**. Tax-free up to £30,000 (with other termination payments). |
| **Notice** | Contractual notice or statutory minimum (s.86), or pay in lieu. |
| **Time off to look for work or arrange training** (s.52) | Employees with 2 years' service under notice of redundancy: reasonable paid time off, pay capped at 40% of a week's pay for the whole notice period. |
| **Trial period** in a new role (s.138) | 4 weeks (longer by written agreement for retraining); leaving during the trial for good reason keeps the redundancy pay right. |
| **Refusing suitable alternative employment** (s.141) | Unreasonable refusal of an offer of suitable alternative employment loses the statutory redundancy payment. |
| **Employer insolvency** (Part XII) | Claim statutory redundancy pay, notice pay (up to 12 weeks), arrears of pay (8 weeks) and holiday (6 weeks) from the Redundancy Payments Service, subject to the £751 weekly cap. |
| **Lay-off and short-time** (ss.147–154) | After 4 consecutive weeks (or 6 in 13), the employee can claim redundancy pay by following the notice procedure. **Guarantee pay** for workless days: up to **£41 a day** for 5 days in 3 months (s.31, from 6 April 2026). |

Claims: written claim to the employer or tribunal claim within **6 months** of the relevant date (s.164), extendable to 12 months.

## 4. Collective redundancy consultation (TULRCA s.188)

**Trigger**: an employer proposing to dismiss as redundant **20 or more employees at one establishment** within **90 days** or less. The ERA 2025 adds an alternative organisation-wide **threshold number** (s.29, new s.195A): in force only for regulation-making purposes; the threshold and start date (expected April 2027) are still to be set — until then, the "one establishment" test applies (see the markers in the TULRCA text).

**Timing**: consultation must begin **in good time** and at least **30 days** before the first dismissal takes effect (20–99 employees) or **45 days** (100 or more).

**With whom**: representatives of a **recognised trade union**; otherwise **employee representatives** (existing body with authority, or elected for the purpose under s.188A). If employees fail to elect after a proper invitation, information goes directly to each affected employee.

**Content**: consultation must include ways of **avoiding** the dismissals, **reducing** the numbers and **mitigating** the consequences, and be undertaken **with a view to reaching agreement** (s.188(2)). The employer must disclose in writing (s.188(4)): reasons for the proposals; numbers and descriptions of employees proposed for dismissal; total number of employees of that description at the establishment; proposed selection method; proposed method of carrying out the dismissals and timing; method of calculating any non-statutory redundancy payments; information on agency workers.

**HR1**: the employer must notify the Secretary of State (Redundancy Payments Service, form HR1) at least 30 or 45 days before the first dismissal (s.193); failure is a criminal offence.

**Protective award** (s.189): if the employer fails to consult, the tribunal may award up to **180 days' gross pay per affected employee** (no week's-pay cap) for dismissals on or after **6 April 2026** (90 days before). The award is punitive; the starting point for no consultation at all is the maximum (Susie Radin v GMB [2004] EWCA Civ 180). Claims by the union, the employee representatives or the employees, within the time limit (6 months for relevant dates from 1 October 2026).

**Special circumstances** defence: rarely successful (not merely insolvency).

**Representatives' rights**: paid time off, facilities, protection against detriment and dismissal (ERA ss.47, 61, 103) — see `60-representatives/`.

## 5. Questions to ask an employee facing redundancy

1. How many are at risk, in which establishment, over what period? (collective duties?)
2. Has there been genuine consultation, and when did it start?
3. What is the pool and the selection criteria? Request the scores and the scoring guidance.
4. Were alternative roles offered? Were they suitable? Trial period?
5. Is there an enhanced contractual or customary redundancy scheme? (Customary enhanced pay can become contractual if clear, consistently applied and known.)
6. Any protected characteristic, pregnancy/family leave, whistleblowing or union activity in the background?
7. Is a settlement agreement being offered? Independent advice is required for it to be binding (employer often contributes to the fee).
