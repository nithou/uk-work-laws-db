# Whistleblowing (public interest disclosure)

## 1. Who is protected

**Workers** (wide definition in s.43K, including agency workers, some contractors, NHS practitioners and trainees; NHS job applicants are protected against discrimination because they appear to be whistleblowers (s.49B)) are protected against detriment; **employees** are protected against dismissal (automatically unfair, no qualifying period, **no cap on compensation**).

## 2. Qualifying disclosure (s.43B)

A disclosure of **information** (not a mere allegation devoid of facts — Kilraine v Wandsworth [2018] EWCA Civ 1436) which, in the **reasonable belief** of the worker, is made **in the public interest** and tends to show one or more of:
- a criminal offence;
- breach of a legal obligation (including the worker's own contract — but then the public interest test matters: Chesterton Global v Nurmohamed [2017] EWCA Civ 979);
- a miscarriage of justice;
- danger to health or safety;
- damage to the environment;
- **sexual harassment** (added from 6 April 2026 by ERA 2025 s.23);
- deliberate concealment of any of the above.

The belief need only be reasonable, not correct. Motive is irrelevant to protection (bad faith can reduce compensation by up to 25%).

## 3. Protected routes

| Route | Condition |
|---|---|
| Employer (or person responsible) — s.43C | Qualifying disclosure is enough |
| Legal adviser — s.43D | In the course of obtaining legal advice |
| Minister (Crown/public bodies) — s.43E | — |
| **Prescribed person** (regulator: HSE, FCA, HMRC, CQC, ICO, EHRC, MPs…) — s.43F | Reasonable belief the matter is within its remit and information substantially true |
| Wider disclosure (media, police…) — s.43G | Reasonable belief substantially true, not for personal gain, and a precondition (fear of detriment, evidence concealment, already raised internally) and reasonable in all the circumstances |
| Exceptionally serious failure — s.43H | Exceptionally serious, reasonable in all the circumstances |

## 4. Protection

- **Detriment** (s.47B): no detriment by the employer, a co-worker or an agent "on the ground that" the worker made a protected disclosure (material influence test — Fecitt v NHS Manchester [2011] EWCA Civ 1190). Employer vicariously liable for co-workers unless it took all reasonable steps.
- **Dismissal** (s.103A): reason or principal reason. The tribunal can look behind a decision-maker manipulated by a manager who knew of the disclosure (Royal Mail v Jhuti [2019] UKSC 55).
- **Selection for redundancy** because of a disclosure: automatically unfair (s.105(6A)).
- **Interim relief**: application within **7 days** of the EDT (s.128) — the tribunal can order the contract to continue until the hearing if the claim is "likely to succeed".
- Confidentiality clauses (including in settlement agreements) are void so far as they prevent a protected disclosure (s.43J).

## 5. Remedies

Uncapped compensation for dismissal; detriment compensation including injury to feelings (Vento bands apply by analogy). Time limit 6 months (relevant dates from 1 October 2026) from the act or the last in a series; Acas early conciliation (not needed for interim relief).

## 6. Practical tips

- Put concerns in writing, factual, and say why you think they matter to the public (e.g. affect clients, patients, the public purse, safety).
- Keep copies of what you raised and when; do not take confidential data you are not entitled to — disclose only what is necessary.
- Use internal whistleblowing policies where possible; consider regulators listed in the Public Interest Disclosure (Prescribed Persons) Order 2014.
- Advice: Protect (whistleblowing charity), trade union, solicitor.
