# Template letters (workers and representatives)

Keep letters factual, dated and polite. Replace [brackets]. Do not include anything untrue.

## 1. Formal grievance

> Dear [Name],
>
> **Formal grievance**
>
> I am raising a formal grievance under [the company's grievance procedure / the Acas Code of Practice on disciplinary and grievance procedures].
>
> My complaint concerns: [summary]. In particular: [numbered, dated facts — who, what, when, witnesses].
>
> This has affected me by [impact: health, pay, career].
>
> I would like the following outcome: [e.g. investigation, payment of £x owed, adjustments, change of line manager, an apology].
>
> I wish to be accompanied at the grievance meeting by [name, colleague / trade union representative] (s.10 Employment Relations Act 1999). Please let me have copies of any documents relied on.
>
> Yours sincerely, [Name]

## 2. Appeal against dismissal or a disciplinary sanction

> **Appeal against [dismissal / final written warning] dated [date]**
>
> I appeal against the decision on the following grounds: (1) the investigation was inadequate because […]; (2) the evidence does not support the finding that […]; (3) the procedure was unfair because […]; (4) the sanction was too severe given my [length of service, clean record, mitigation, comparison with how others were treated]; (5) new evidence: […].
>
> I request a hearing and wish to be accompanied by [name]. Please send me the notes of all meetings and witness statements.

## 3. Request for written reasons for dismissal (s.92 ERA 1996)

> Please provide a written statement of the reasons for my dismissal within 14 days, as required by section 92 of the Employment Rights Act 1996.

## 4. Subject access request (UK GDPR, Article 15)

> I make a subject access request for all personal data you hold about me, including emails, messages, notes, investigation documents, performance records and any data relating to [specific matter / named individuals] from [date] to date, together with the information required by Article 15(1). Please respond within one month.

## 5. Unpaid wages / holiday pay

> I have not been paid [amount] due for [period / holiday / commission]. Under my contract [clause] and section 13 of the Employment Rights Act 1996 [and regulation 16 of the Working Time Regulations 1998], this sum is properly payable. Please pay it in my next pay run or by [date]. Otherwise I will contact Acas for early conciliation.

## 6. Flexible working request (ERA s.80F)

> This is a statutory request for flexible working. I request [the change: hours/days/location] to start from [date]. [Optional: how I think it could work.] I have made [no / one] other request in the last 12 months. I look forward to discussing this with you; please consult me before reaching a decision, which must be made within two months.

## 7. Letter of resignation in response to a fundamental breach (constructive dismissal)

> I resign with [immediate effect / notice] in response to your repudiatory breach of my contract of employment, in particular [list: e.g. unilateral pay cut on (date); failure to deal with my grievance about bullying dated (date); …], which, individually and cumulatively, have destroyed the trust and confidence between us. I do not affirm the contract. I consider that I have been constructively dismissed.

(Take advice first: resigning is a risk.)

## 8. Without prejudice proposal

> **Without prejudice and subject to contract**
>
> I propose that my employment ends on [date] on the following terms: [payment, notice, reference, …]. I would need independent legal advice for a settlement agreement and ask you to contribute £[amount] plus VAT to its cost.

## 9. Representatives — request for information for collective redundancy consultation

> As [trade union / elected employee] representatives, we request the information required by section 188(4) TULRCA 1992: the reasons for the proposals; the numbers and descriptions of employees proposed for dismissal; the total number of employees of each description at the establishment; the proposed selection method; the proposed method and timing of dismissals; the method of calculating redundancy payments; information about agency workers. Consultation must cover ways of avoiding dismissals, reducing numbers and mitigating consequences, with a view to reaching agreement. We also ask for [business case, financial information, list of vacancies].

## 10. Representatives — request for time off and facilities

> Under section [168 / 168A / 168B / 170] TULRCA 1992 and the Acas Code of Practice on time off for trade union duties and activities, I request paid time off on [dates] for [purpose], and access to [room, computer, noticeboard, email list] for this purpose [s.168(3A)].

## 11. Health and safety concern / refusal to work in serious and imminent danger

> I am raising a health and safety concern about [hazard] at [location]. I reasonably believe it presents [a risk to health and safety / serious and imminent danger]. [I have left / refuse to return to] the area until it is made safe (s.44(1)(d) and s.100(1)(d) ERA 1996). Please confirm the action you will take. I have copied this to [the safety representative / HSE].

## 12. Whistleblowing disclosure

> I wish to make a disclosure under [the whistleblowing policy]. I have information that [facts], which I believe tends to show [a breach of legal obligation / danger to health and safety / criminal offence]. I believe this is in the public interest because [reason]. I ask that it be investigated and that I am protected from any detriment for raising it.
