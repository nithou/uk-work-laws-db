# Worker – Employment status

_Source: Acas — https://www.acas.org.uk/employment-status/worker-employment-status_

People with 'worker' employment status have some employment rights, but not as many as employees.

## What makes someone a worker

Someone is likely to have the legal status of worker if most of these things apply:

- their work for the organisation is more casual, for example work is less structured or they do not have a regular working pattern
- they're usually required to personally do the work
- they're not offered regular or guaranteed hours by the employer
- they have very little obligation to make themselves available for work, but should do work they've agreed to
- they're not carrying out work for a client or customer of their own

If most of these do not apply, someone is more likely to be an [employee](https://www.acas.org.uk/employment-status/employee-employment-status "Employee") or [self-employed](https://www.acas.org.uk/employment-status/self-employment "Self-employment").

Workers are sometimes referred to as 'limb (b)' workers. This term comes from the Employment Rights Act 1996.

## Employment rights

Workers' rights include:

- a [written statement of employment particulars](https://www.acas.org.uk/what-must-be-written-in-an-employment-contract "Written statements") outlining their job rights and responsibilities
- [minimum wage](https://www.acas.org.uk/national-minimum-wage-entitlement "National Minimum Wage")
- [paid holiday](https://www.acas.org.uk/checking-holiday-entitlement "Holiday entitlement")
- [payslips](https://www.acas.org.uk/payslips "Payslips")
- protection for [whistleblowing](https://www.acas.org.uk/whistleblowing-at-work "Whistleblowing at work")
- protection against [discrimination](https://www.acas.org.uk/discrimination-and-the-law "Discrimination at work")
- protection from [less favourable treatment for working part time](https://www.acas.org.uk/part-time-workers/if-someone-who-works-part-time-is-treated-less-favourably "If someone's treated less favourably")
- [protection against detriment for taking action over a health and safety issue](https://www.acas.org.uk/health-and-safety-at-work/protection-from-dismissal-and-detriment "Protection from dismissal and detriment")

Workers are not usually entitled to:

- a minimum [notice period](https://www.acas.org.uk/notice-periods "Notice periods") if their employment is ending, for example if their employer is dismissing them
- protection against [unfair dismissal](https://www.acas.org.uk/dismissals/unfair-dismissal "Unfair dismissal")
- make [statutory flexible working requests](https://www.acas.org.uk/statutory-flexible-working-requests "Statutory flexible working requests")
- [time off for dependants](https://www.acas.org.uk/time-off-for-dependants "Time off for dependants")
- statutory [redundancy pay](https://www.acas.org.uk/your-rights-during-redundancy/redundancy-pay "Redundancy pay")

## Statutory pay and leave

Depending on their National Insurance contributions, workers might also be entitled to:

- [statutory sick pay (SSP)](https://www.acas.org.uk/checking-sick-pay/statutory-sick-pay-ssp "Statutory sick pay")
- [shared parental pay](https://www.acas.org.uk/shared-parental-leave-and-pay/shared-parental-pay "Shared parental pay")
- [parental bereavement pay](https://www.acas.org.uk/parental-bereavement-leave-and-pay "Parental bereavement leave and pay")
- [maternity pay](https://www.acas.org.uk/statutory-maternity-leave-and-pay/eligibility-for-pay "Eligibility for pay")
- [paternity pay](https://www.acas.org.uk/paternity-rights-leave-and-pay/paternity-pay "Statutory paternity pay")
- [adoption pay](https://www.acas.org.uk/your-adoption-leave-pay-and-other-rights "Adoption leave and pay")
- [neonatal care pay](https://www.acas.org.uk/neonatal-care-leave-and-pay "Neonatal care leave and pay")

Workers are not entitled to:

- shared parental leave
- parental bereavement leave
- maternity, paternity and adoption leave
- neonatal care leave
- carer's leave
- bereaved partner's paternity leave

This is because they do not have to make themselves available for work. They can choose to take time off when they want to.
