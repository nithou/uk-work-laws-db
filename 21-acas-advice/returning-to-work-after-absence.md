# Return to work meetings – Returning to work after absence

_Source: Acas — https://www.acas.org.uk/returning-to-work-after-absence_

When someone returns to work after an absence, they should talk with their employer. This is to make sure they're ready to return and have any support they need.

An employer should have a process they follow when someone returns to work. This should be in the organisation's absence policy, if they have one.

A return to work meeting is often just an informal chat between a worker and their manager. These meetings are sometimes called return to work interviews.

## What a return to work meeting is for

It's not a legal requirement to have a return to work meeting. But a chat as soon as possible after a worker has returned is a good opportunity to:

- make sure the worker is ready to return to work
- talk about any work updates that happened while they were off
- see if they need any support
- agree on a plan for returning to work, if appropriate, for example a [phased return to work](https://www.acas.org.uk/returning-to-work-after-absence/phased-return-to-work "Phased returns")
- talk about what they would like other workers to know about their absence and what they would like to keep confidential

### If the worker has been off sick

If the worker has been off sick, their employer might also:

- look at any recommendations from the worker's doctor
- consider a referral to a medical service such as occupational health

If the worker is disabled, their employer should review their [reasonable adjustments](https://www.acas.org.uk/reasonable-adjustments "Reasonable adjustments at work"). They should discuss whether they need to make further reasonable adjustments to support their return to work.

[Use our return to work meeting template for when an worker has been off sick](https://www.acas.org.uk/return-to-work-meeting-template "Return to work meeting template")

### Looking out for causes of absence

An employer should also look out for signs of underlying problems causing an worker's absence. For example:

- work-related stress
- a disability

The employer should be sensitive and understanding if they're talking to someone about this. A worker does not have to tell them anything. But talking openly can help the employer put the right support in place.

Find out more about:

- [managing work-related stress](https://www.acas.org.uk/managing-work-related-stress "Managing work-related stress")
- [supporting disabled people at work](https://www.acas.org.uk/supporting-disabled-people "Supporting disabled people at work")

## How to prepare for a return to work meeting

Before a return to work meeting, an employer should:

- make sure the chat will be private and confidential, whether it is face to face or online
- gather any relevant worker records – for example, records of previous conversations about the worker's absence
- prepare questions to ask
- make sure they know what support they can offer

They should also think about how they might respond to any requests from the worker. For example, they might be eligible for:

- [flexible working](https://www.acas.org.uk/flexible-working "Flexible working")
- [phased return to work](https://www.acas.org.uk/returning-to-work-after-absence/phased-return-to-work "Phased returns")
- [reasonable adjustments](https://www.acas.org.uk/reasonable-adjustments "Reasonable adjustments at work") – if the worker is disabled

If the worker has been off sick, an employer should read any recommendations from the worker's doctor in a:

- [fit note](https://www.acas.org.uk/fit-notes-and-proof-of-sickness "Fit notes when off sick")
- [report about their health](https://www.acas.org.uk/getting-a-doctors-report-about-an-employees-health "Getting a doctor's report about a worker's health")

Before the return to work meeting, a worker should speak to their employer to:

- find out more about the meeting and what to expect
- check if there's anything they need to bring

They should also think about:

- any changes or adjustments they might need
- what support might help in the future

## If a worker is unhappy with how their return was handled

A worker might be unhappy with how their return to work has been handled. In these cases, they should [raise it with their employer](https://www.acas.org.uk/how-to-raise-a-problem-at-work "Talking with your employer about a problem").
