# The Employment Tribunal Procedure Rules 2024 — Part 11 — Decisions and reasons

_Revised text as published on legislation.gov.uk (version valid from 2026-03-02). Check the annotations and the list of amendments not yet applied below: some changes made by later legislation (notably the Employment Rights Act 2025) may not be reflected in the text yet._

### Rule 58 — Decisions made without a hearing
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1155/rule/58) · extent: E+W+S

Decisions made without a hearing must be communicated in writing to the parties, identifying the member or members of the Tribunal who have made the decision, or in the case of a decision made by a member of staff under rule 7(1) (delegation to staff), identifying the member of staff who has made the decision.

### Rule 59 — Decisions made at or following a hearing
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1155/rule/59) · extent: E+W+S

(1) Where there is a hearing the Tribunal may either announce its decision in relation to any issue at the hearing or reserve it to be sent to the parties as soon as practicable in writing.
(2) If the decision is announced at the hearing, a written record of the decision must be provided to the parties.
(3) Paragraph (2) does not apply to decisions concerned only with the conduct of a hearing unless a party requests that a specific decision is so recorded.
(4) The written record must be approved by the presiding member.

### Rule 60 — Reasons
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1155/rule/60) · extent: E+W+S

(1) The Tribunal must give reasons for its decision on any disputed issue, whether substantive or procedural, including any decision on an application for reconsideration or for an order for costs, preparation time or wasted costs.
(2) In the case of a decision given in writing the reasons must also be given in writing.
(3) In the case of a decision announced at a hearing, the reasons may be given orally at the hearing or reserved to be given in writing which may, but need not, be as part of the written record of the decision.
(4) In respect of a case management order, where reasons have been given orally at a hearing, the presiding member must announce that written reasons will not be provided unless requested by any party—
  (a) at the hearing, or
  (b) by a written request received by the Tribunal within 14 days of the sending of the written record of the decision,
and the written record of the decision must repeat that information.
(4A) In respect of a judgment—
  (a) where reasons are given in writing under paragraph (2) or reserved to be given in writing under paragraph (3), those reasons must be full reasons;
  (b) where reasons are given orally at a hearing under paragraph (3), those reasons may be either—
    (i) summary reasons, or
    (ii) full reasons.
(4B) Where summary reasons are given under paragraph (4A)(b)(i), the presiding member must announce that the reasons given are summary reasons and that written summary reasons will not be provided unless requested by any party—
  (a) at the hearing, or
  (b) by a written request received by the Tribunal within 14 days of the sending of the written record of the decision,
and the written record of the decision must repeat that information.
(4C) Where written summary reasons are provided further to a request under paragraph (4B), they must state that written full reasons will not be provided unless requested by any party by a written request received by the Tribunal within 14 days of the sending of the written summary reasons.
(4D) Where full reasons are given under paragraph (4A)(b)(ii), the presiding member must announce that written full reasons will not be provided unless requested by any party—
  (a) at the hearing, or
  (b) by a written request received by the Tribunal within 14 days of the sending of the written record of the decision,
and the written record of the decision must repeat that information.
(4E) If the Tribunal receives a request for written summary reasons in accordance with paragraph (4B), the Tribunal may, if it considers it appropriate to do so, provide written full reasons.
(5) If the Tribunal does not receive a request for written reasons under paragraph (4) , (4C) or (4D), the Tribunal must provide written reasons for a case management order or written full reasons for a judgment only if requested to do so by the Employment Appeal Tribunal or a court.
(6) The reasons given for any decision must be proportionate to the significance of the issue and for case management orders may be very short.
(7) In this rule—
- “full reasons” are reasons which— 1. identify the issues which the Tribunal has determined, 2. state the findings of fact made in relation to those issues, 3. concisely identify the relevant law, and state how that law has been applied to those findings in order to decide the issues, and 4. where the judgment includes a financial award, identify by means of a table or otherwise, how the amount to be paid has been calculated;
- “summary reasons” are reasons which provide a brief explanation of why the Tribunal has reached its decision in respect of each issue.
(8) Written reasons must be approved by the presiding member, other than written reasons for decisions made by a member of staff under rule 7(1) (delegation to staff).

### Rule 61 — Absence of presiding member
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1155/rule/61) · extent: E+W+S

(1) If it is impossible or not practicable for the written record or written reasons to be approved by the presiding member as a result of death, incapacity or absence, it must be approved—
  (a) in the case of a Tribunal composed of more than one member, by the other member or members;
  (b) in the case of a Tribunal composed of a single member, by the relevant post-holder.
(2) In this rule, the relevant post-holder is—
  (a) in England and Wales, the President (England and Wales) or a Regional Employment Judge;
  (b) in Scotland, the President (Scotland) or the Vice President.

### Rule 62 — Consent orders and judgments
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1155/rule/62) · extent: E+W+S

If the parties agree in writing or orally at a hearing upon the terms of any order or judgment the Tribunal may, if it thinks fit, make such order or judgment, in which case it must be identified as having been made by consent.

<details><summary>Annotations</summary>

- Commencement: Rule 62 in force at 6.1.2025 immediately after S.I. 2024/1156 comes into force, see rule 1(1)

</details>

### Rule 63 — When a judgment or order takes effect
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1155/rule/63) · extent: E+W+S

A judgment or order takes effect from the day when it is given or made, or on such later date as is specified by the Tribunal.

<details><summary>Annotations</summary>

- Commencement: Rule 63 in force at 6.1.2025 immediately after S.I. 2024/1156 comes into force, see rule 1(1)

</details>

### Rule 64 — Time for compliance
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1155/rule/64) · extent: E+W+S

A party must comply with a judgment or order for the payment of an amount of money within 14 days of the date of the judgment or order, unless—
  (a) the judgment, order, or any of these Rules specifies a different date for compliance, or
  (b) the Tribunal has stayed (or in Scotland sisted) the proceedings or judgment.

<details><summary>Annotations</summary>

- Commencement: Rule 64 in force at 6.1.2025 immediately after S.I. 2024/1156 comes into force, see rule 1(1)

</details>

### Rule 65 — The Register
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1155/rule/65) · extent: E+W+S

(1) Subject to paragraph (2) and rules 49 (privacy and restrictions on disclosure) and 93 (national security proceedings), a copy of any judgment and any written full reasons for a judgment must be entered into the Register.
(2) Paragraph (1) does not apply to—
  (a) judgments for withdrawn claims under rule 51 (dismissal following withdrawal);
  (b) a refusal of an application for reconsideration of a judgment under rule 70(2) (process for reconsideration) where the Tribunal considers that there is no reasonable prospect of the judgment being varied or revoked on the grounds that substantially the same application for reconsideration has already been made and refused.

### Rule 66 — Copies of decisions for referring court
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1155/rule/66) · extent: E+W+S

Where the proceedings were referred to the Tribunal by a court, the Tribunal must send a copy of any decision and any written reasons to that court.

### Rule 67 — Correction of clerical mistakes and accidental slips
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1155/rule/67) · extent: E+W+S

(1) The Tribunal may at any time correct any clerical mistake or other accidental slip or omission in any order, judgment or other document produced by the Tribunal.
(2) If a correction is made under this rule, any published version of the document must also be corrected.
(3) If any document is corrected by the Tribunal under this rule, a copy of the corrected version, approved by the presiding member of that Tribunal or in accordance with rule 61 (absence of presiding member), must be sent to all the parties.
(4) If any document is corrected by a member of staff in accordance with rule 7(1) (delegation to staff) and this rule, a copy of the corrected version, approved by that member of staff, must be sent to all the parties.
