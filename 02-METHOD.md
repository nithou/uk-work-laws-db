# Method: how to reason about a UK employment law question

## 1. Sources and their weight

| Source | Role | Where in the corpus |
|---|---|---|
| Acts of Parliament (ERA 1996, Equality Act 2010, TULRCA 1992, ERA 2025, NMWA 1998…) | Statutory rights; the floor that cannot be contracted out of (ERA s.203, EqA s.144) except by a valid settlement agreement or Acas COT3 | `10-acts/` |
| Statutory instruments (WTR 1998, TUPE 2006, family leave regs, ET Rules 2024…) | Detail of many rights; procedure | `11-regulations-and-rules/` |
| Retained EU-derived law | Since 1 January 2024, "assimilated law" (Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023): EU principles of supremacy and general principles no longer apply; earlier CJEU case law still persuasive unless departed from by higher UK courts | — |
| Statutory Codes of Practice (Acas, EHRC) | Not binding, but tribunals must take them into account; Acas disciplinary and grievance Code: award adjustment up to 25% (TULRCA s.207A) | `20-acas-codes-of-practice/` |
| Case law (Supreme Court, Court of Appeal / Court of Session, EAT) | Binding interpretation; key tests (Burchell, band of reasonable responses, Polkey, Western Excavating, Malik…) | `70-case-law/` |
| Contract of employment, express and implied terms, collective agreements incorporated into contracts, handbooks | Contractual rights, often more generous than statute | Ask the user |
| Official guidance (Acas, GOV.UK) | Plain-language explanation | `21-`, `22-` |

There are no sector-wide collective agreements with legal effect comparable to French "conventions collectives": collective agreements are presumed not legally enforceable between union and employer (TULRCA s.179) but their terms can be **incorporated** into individual contracts. The Employment Rights Act 2025 introduces Fair Pay Agreements for adult social care (negotiating body) and the School Support Staff Negotiating Body.

## 2. Employment status decides which rights apply

| Status | Test (summary) | Main rights |
|---|---|---|
| **Employee** (ERA s.230(1)) | Contract of service: personal service, mutuality of obligation, control, and other factors consistent with employment (Ready Mixed Concrete 1968; Autoclenz v Belcher 2011 — look at the reality, not the label) | All rights: unfair dismissal, redundancy pay, statutory notice, family leave, flexible working, TUPE, plus worker rights |
| **Worker** (ERA s.230(3)(b); "limb (b)") | Personal service for someone who is not a client or customer of a business carried on by the individual (Uber v Aslam [2021] UKSC 5; Pimlico Plumbers v Smith [2018] UKSC 29) | National minimum wage, working time and paid holiday, protection from unlawful deductions, whistleblowing, trade union rights, right to be accompanied, SSP (via employed earner status), part-time worker protection, Equality Act |
| **Self-employed** (genuine) | In business on own account | Equality Act protection if "employment" under EqA s.83 (contract personally to do work — wider); health and safety |

Agency workers: rights under the Agency Workers Regulations 2010 (day-one access to facilities and job vacancies; equal basic pay and conditions after 12 weeks). Zero-hours workers: exclusivity clauses unenforceable (ERA s.27A); guaranteed-hours and shift-notice rights under ERA 2025 not yet in force.

## 3. Continuous employment and qualifying periods

Many rights require a period of **continuous employment** (ERA Part XIV, Chapter I, ss.210–219): weeks count if governed by a contract of employment; some gaps preserve continuity (sickness, temporary cessation of work, arrangement or custom); TUPE transfers preserve continuity. Typical qualifying periods (as at November 2026):

| Right | Qualifying period |
|---|---|
| Unfair dismissal (ordinary) | **2 years** if the effective date of termination (EDT) is before 1 January 2027; **6 months** if the EDT is on or after 1 January 2027 (ERA 2025 s.25; SI 2026/559 reg. 4) |
| Automatically unfair reasons (whistleblowing, pregnancy, health and safety, trade union, assertion of statutory right, etc.) | None (day one) |
| Written reasons for dismissal on request | 2 years (ERA s.92), any length if dismissed while pregnant or on maternity/adoption leave |
| Statutory redundancy payment | 2 years |
| Statutory minimum notice from employer | 1 month |
| Paternity leave, unpaid parental leave | **Day one** from 6 April 2026 (ERA 2025 ss.15–17) |
| Statutory paternity pay, SMP | 26 weeks by the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth + earnings test |
| Flexible working request | Day one (since 6 April 2024) |
| SSP | Day one, no lower earnings limit, payable from the first day of sickness (from 6 April 2026) |
| Discrimination, NMW, holiday, deductions, whistleblowing | None |

## 4. Territorial and jurisdiction points

- Great Britain: England, Wales and Scotland share the employment statutes and the employment tribunal system (Employment Tribunals (England and Wales) and Employment Tribunals (Scotland)); appeals on points of law go to the **Employment Appeal Tribunal**, then the Court of Appeal (E&W) or the Court of Session (Scotland), then the Supreme Court.
- **Northern Ireland**: separate legislation and industrial tribunals; the Labour Relations Agency replaces Acas. Not covered here.
- Overseas workers: the right not to be unfairly dismissed covers employees working in Great Britain and some working abroad with a sufficiently strong connection (Lawson v Serco [2006] UKHL 3; Ravat v Halliburton [2012] UKSC 1).

## 5. Checklist for every question

1. **Who?** Status, length of service, age (redundancy pay, NMW band), protected characteristics, union membership or representative role.
2. **What happened and when?** Exact dates; whether the employment continues or ended; EDT.
3. **What rules apply?** Statute (version in force at the date) → contract and policies (often better) → collective agreements → custom and practice.
4. **What process was followed?** Investigation, invitation, right to be accompanied (Employment Relations Act 1999 s.10), hearing, written outcome, appeal — compare with the Acas Code.
5. **Evidence?** Contract, payslips, emails, notes of meetings, witness names, medical evidence, comparators.
6. **Remedies and risk?** Compensation, Acas uplift, Polkey and contributory fault reductions, costs risk, time and stress.
7. **Next step?** Grievance or appeal internally; Acas early conciliation (mandatory before most claims); ET1; settlement.

## 6. Frequent traps

- Assuming a time limit runs from the end of the appeal: it runs from the **act** or the **EDT** (an internal appeal does not stop the clock).
- Waiting for the outcome of a grievance before contacting Acas: early conciliation must start within the time limit.
- Treating "gross misconduct" as automatically fair: the employer must still act reasonably (investigation, procedure, sanction within the band of reasonable responses).
- Signing a settlement agreement without independent legal advice: it would not be binding on statutory claims (ERA s.203(3)), but it may still bind on contractual matters.
- Forgetting that holiday pay and SSP are statutory minimums and the contract may give more.
- Confusing **wrongful dismissal** (breach of contract — failure to give notice) with **unfair dismissal** (statutory fairness).
