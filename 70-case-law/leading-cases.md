# Leading cases (Great Britain)

Abbreviations: UKSC Supreme Court; UKHL House of Lords; EWCA Court of Appeal; CSIH Inner House of the Court of Session; EAT Employment Appeal Tribunal; ICR Industrial Cases Reports; IRLR Industrial Relations Law Reports.

## Employment status

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| Ready Mixed Concrete v Minister of Pensions [1968] 2 QB 497 | Classic test of a contract of service: personal service, control, other provisions consistent with employment |
| Autoclenz Ltd v Belcher [2011] UKSC 41 | Tribunals look at the true agreement, not sham written terms (substitution clauses) |
| Pimlico Plumbers Ltd v Smith [2018] UKSC 29 | Limited right of substitution consistent with personal service; worker status |
| Uber BV v Aslam [2021] UKSC 5 | Worker status depends on statutory purpose and reality of the relationship; drivers were workers; working time when logged on and ready |
| Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain v CAC (Deliveroo) [2023] UKSC 43 | Genuine unfettered substitution right meant riders were not in an employment relationship for Article 11 collective bargaining |

## Unfair dismissal

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| British Home Stores v Burchell [1978] IRLR 379 (EAT) | Misconduct: genuine belief, reasonable grounds, reasonable investigation |
| Iceland Frozen Foods v Jones [1983] ICR 17 (EAT) | Band of reasonable responses |
| Post Office v Foley; HSBC v Madden [2000] ICR 1283 (EWCA) | Band of reasonable responses reaffirmed; tribunal must not substitute its view |
| Sainsbury's Supermarkets v Hitt [2002] EWCA Civ 1588 | The band applies to the investigation |
| Polkey v AE Dayton Services [1987] UKHL 8 | Procedural failings make a dismissal unfair; compensation reduced to reflect the chance of a fair dismissal anyway |
| Taylor v OCS Group [2006] EWCA Civ 702 | An appeal can cure earlier procedural defects; look at the process as a whole |
| A v B [2003] IRLR 405; Salford Royal NHS Trust v Roldan [2010] EWCA Civ 522 | More careful investigation needed where consequences for career are serious |
| Royal Mail Group v Jhuti [2019] UKSC 55 | Where a manager hides the real reason behind an invented one adopted by an innocent decision-maker, the hidden reason can be attributed to the employer |
| East Lindsey DC v Daubney [1977] ICR 566 (EAT) | Ill-health dismissal requires consultation and medical evidence |
| BS v Dundee City Council [2013] CSIH 91 | Long-term absence: whether the employer can be expected to wait longer |
| Williams v Compair Maxam [1982] ICR 156 (EAT) | Fair redundancy: warning, consultation, objective selection, alternatives |
| Murray v Foyle Meats [1999] UKHL 30 | Redundancy test: has the need for employees diminished, and is the dismissal attributable to it |
| Lawson v Serco [2006] UKHL 3 | Territorial reach of unfair dismissal |

## Constructive and wrongful dismissal, contract

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| Western Excavating (ECC) v Sharp [1978] QB 761 (EWCA) | Constructive dismissal requires a repudiatory breach (contract test, not unreasonableness) |
| Malik v BCCI [1997] UKHL 23 | Implied term of mutual trust and confidence |
| Kaur v Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust [2018] EWCA Civ 978 | "Last straw" and affirmation; the questions to ask |
| Johnson v Unisys [2001] UKHL 13; Eastwood v Magnox [2004] UKHL 35 | No common-law damages for the manner of dismissal (Johnson exclusion zone), but pre-dismissal breaches actionable |
| Geys v Société Générale [2012] UKSC 63 | Elective theory: repudiation ends the contract only when accepted; payment in lieu clauses must be clearly exercised |
| Braganza v BP Shipping [2015] UKSC 17 | Contractual discretion must be exercised rationally and in good faith |

## Discrimination

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| Igen v Wong [2005] EWCA Civ 142; Efobi v Royal Mail [2021] UKSC 33 | Two-stage burden of proof |
| Madarassy v Nomura [2007] EWCA Civ 33 | Difference in status and treatment not enough to shift the burden |
| Archibald v Fife Council [2004] UKHL 32 | Reasonable adjustments can require positive treatment, including redeployment |
| Essop v Home Office; Naeem v Secretary of State for Justice [2017] UKSC 27 | Indirect discrimination: no need to show why the PCP disadvantages the group |
| Chief Constable of West Yorkshire v Homer [2012] UKSC 15 | Justification of age-related PCPs |
| Seldon v Clarkson Wright & Jakes [2012] UKSC 16 | Direct age discrimination justification (legitimate social policy aims) |
| Grainger plc v Nicholson [2010] ICR 360 (EAT); Forstater v CGD Europe [2022] ICR 1 (EAT) | Philosophical belief test; gender-critical belief protected (manifestation can still be limited) |
| Higgs v Farmor's School [2025] EWCA Civ 109 | Dismissal for manifesting a protected belief must be proportionate |
| For Women Scotland Ltd v The Scottish Ministers [2025] UKSC 16 | "Sex", "woman" and "man" in the Equality Act refer to biological sex |
| Vento v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire (No 2) [2002] EWCA Civ 1871 | Bands for injury to feelings (updated annually by Presidential Guidance) |
| Allay (UK) Ltd v Gehlen [2021] ICR 645 (EAT) | "All reasonable steps" defence: stale training is not enough |
| Richmond Pharmacology v Dhaliwal [2009] ICR 724 (EAT) | Harassment: purpose or effect; context |

## Whistleblowing

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| Chesterton Global v Nurmohamed [2017] EWCA Civ 979 | Public interest test can be met even for disclosures about the worker's own contract if wider interests are affected |
| Kilraine v Wandsworth LBC [2018] EWCA Civ 1436 | Disclosure must contain sufficient factual content |
| Fecitt v NHS Manchester [2011] EWCA Civ 1190 | Detriment: disclosure must materially influence the treatment |
| Timis v Osipov [2018] EWCA Civ 2321 | Individual co-workers liable for detriment amounting to dismissal |

## Pay, holiday, working time

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| King v Sash Window Workshop (CJEU C-214/16, 2017) | Carry-over of leave the employer did not pay for — codified in WTR reg. 13(15)–(16) for GB |
| Bear Scotland v Fulton [2015] ICR 221 (EAT) | Overtime in holiday pay; 3-month gap rule for series of deductions (GB position) |
| Chief Constable of the PSNI v Agnew [2023] UKSC 33 | Series of deductions in NI law not broken by 3-month gaps; holiday pay includes normal overtime (NI case, persuasive in GB) |
| Harpur Trust v Brazel [2022] UKSC 21 | Part-year workers' holiday not pro-rated (pre-2024 law; now replaced by 12.07% accrual for leave years from 1 April 2024) |
| Royal Mencap Society v Tomlinson-Blake [2021] UKSC 8 | Sleep-in shifts: only time awake for the purpose of working counts for NMW |
| Kostal UK Ltd v Dunkley [2021] UKSC 47 | Unlawful inducement to bypass collective bargaining (TULRCA s.145B) |
| Mercer v Alternative Future Group [2024] UKSC 12 | Declaration of incompatibility: lack of protection against detriment for taking part in lawful strike action (now addressed by ERA 2025) |
| Tesco Stores v USDAW [2024] UKSC 28 | Implied term preventing fire and rehire to remove a contractual retained pay benefit |

## Procedure and access to justice

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| R (Unison) v Lord Chancellor [2017] UKSC 51 | Employment tribunal fees unlawful |
| Dedman v British Building [1974] ICR 53 (EWCA) | "Not reasonably practicable": reliance on a skilled adviser's error usually does not extend time |
| Abertawe Bro Morgannwg UHB v Morgan [2018] EWCA Civ 640 | "Just and equitable" extension is a broad discretion |
| Selkent Bus Co v Moore [1996] ICR 836 (EAT); Vaughan v Modality Partnership [2021] ICR 535 (EAT) | Amendment applications: balance of hardship and injustice |
| Phoenix House v Stockman [2016] IRLR 848 (EAT) | Covert recordings: admissibility and effect on remedy |
| Susie Radin v GMB [2004] EWCA Civ 180 | Protective award is punitive; start from the maximum where no consultation |
