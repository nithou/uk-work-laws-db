# Employers: recognise a trade union

_Source: GOV.UK — https://www.gov.uk/trade-union-recognition-employers — updated 2015-01-08_

## Overview

As an employer you may need to work with trade unions that represent groups of your workers, sometimes known as [bargaining units](https://www.gov.uk/trade-union-recognition-employers/establish-bargaining-unit).

Trade unions will negotiate with you on working conditions, for example pay and holiday. You need to recognise the trade union before they can negotiate with you.

## How a trade union gets recognition

1. The union must ask you to [recognise them voluntarily](https://www.gov.uk/trade-union-recognition-employers/union-requests-recognition) - if you agree to the request then the union is recognised.
2. If you do not want to recognise the union and have more than 21 workers, they can apply for [statutory recognition](https://www.gov.uk/trade-union-recognition-employers/statutory-recognition) from the Central Arbitration Committee (CAC).

## When the union requests recognition

The union must ask you - the employer - in writing if you’ll agree to recognise them voluntarily.

The written request must:

- give the name of the union
- identify which workers will be represented by the union when it’s recognised, sometimes known as the bargaining unit
- state that the union is making the request under Schedule A1 of the Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992

## Respond to the request

You have 10 working days to respond to the request.

You can:

- agree to recognise the union voluntarily - and begin [collective bargaining](https://www.gov.uk/working-with-trade-unions/collective-bargaining)
- reject the request - the union may then apply for [statutory recognition](https://www.gov.uk/trade-union-recognition-employers/statutory-recognition)
- refuse the initial request but agree to negotiate

## Negotiate with the union

You have 20 working days, or longer if you agree this with the union, to come to an agreement about:

- which workers are in the bargaining unit
- whether the union should be recognised for [collective bargaining](https://www.gov.uk/working-with-trade-unions/collective-bargaining)

You have 10 days to suggest that the Advisory, Conciliation and Arbitration Service (Acas) are brought in to assist with the negotiations.

### What happens next

If you cannot agree, or you’ve agreed the bargaining unit but not recognised the union, the union can apply to the Central Arbitration Committee (CAC) for [statutory recognition](https://www.gov.uk/trade-union-recognition-employers/statutory-recognition).

## When the union applies for statutory recognition

The union can apply to the Central Arbitration Committee (CAC) for ‘statutory recognition’ if you do not agree to recognise them voluntarily.

CAC will accept the trade union’s application for recognition if it meets the requirements, unless you challenge the application.

## Providing information to the CAC

The CAC case manager handling the union’s application will ask you for the following information regarding all workers in the bargaining unit:

- name
- date of birth
- worker category (for example, technical and skilled but not managers)

You should respond to the CAC within 5 working days. If you do not, the CAC may give you a final warning (‘remedial order’) to provide this information. The CAC will still consider the union’s application if you do not provide the information.

## Challenge an application

You can challenge the application if you think it does not meet the requirements.

### Application requirements

The union can apply if:

- they’ve sent you a copy of their application and any supporting documents
- they have at least 10% union membership within the proposed [bargaining unit](https://www.gov.uk/trade-union-recognition-employers/establish-bargaining-unit)

They cannot apply if:

- they’ve applied for recognition in the last 3 years
- they are not a certified independent union
- there’s already a recognition agreement that allows another union to represent workers in the bargaining unit - except in limited circumstances
- another union - representing 10% of the workers in the proposed bargaining unit - has already applied to CAC

### How to complain

CAC will send you a form so you can challenge the application if you think that the union has not met one or more of the requirements.

You have 5 working days to send it to CAC - the address is on the form.

You’ll hear from CAC within 10 working days. They may:

- ask for more information so they can make a decision
- reject the application if the union has not met the requirements
- accept the union’s application

## When the CAC accepts a union’s application

You’ll need to [start discussions](https://www.gov.uk/trade-union-recognition-employers/establish-bargaining-unit) about which workers the union will represent, sometimes known as the bargaining unit.

The union may also tell you that it wants to have [access to the workers](https://www.gov.uk/trade-union-recognition-employers/workplace-access-during-recognition-process).

## Establishing the bargaining unit

The ‘bargaining unit’ is the group of workers that will be represented by the union.

You can agree who is in this unit with the union as part of your negotiations. If you do not, the Central Arbitration Committee (CAC) will decide.

## Providing information to the union and the CAC

If the CAC accepts the union’s application for statutory recognition and you have not agreed on the bargaining unit with them, you must send the CAC and the union:

- a list of the categories of workers who will be in the proposed bargaining unit, for example, technical and skilled but not managers
- a list of the places where they work
- the number of workers in each category at each workplace

You have 5 working days to send this from the date the application is accepted.

## How the CAC decides on the bargaining unit

The CAC will hold a hearing and decide the bargaining unit based on:

- your views and those of the union
- compatibility with effective management of your company
- existing national and bargaining arrangements

## Work with a ‘suitable independent person’ (SIP)

When the bargaining unit has been agreed, the union may ask the CAC to appoint a SIP to communicate with workers in the bargaining unit. If this happens, you must give the SIP the names and addresses of:

- workers in the proposed bargaining unit
- any workers who join or leave the bargaining unit

If you do not provide this information, you’ll get a ‘remedial order’ from the CAC. You must do what the order says or the CAC could declare that you must recognise the union.

## When the bargaining unit has been agreed or decided

The CAC will decide if there needs to be a [ballot of workers](https://www.gov.uk/trade-union-recognition-employers/ballot-union-recognition).

## Workplace access during the recognition process

If the Central Arbitration Committee (CAC) accepts the union’s application for recognition, the union can request access to the workplace to engage with workers. They must do this in writing, within 5 working days. Access can be physical, digital or written.

You and the union then have 15 working days to negotiate an access agreement. This can be extended if you and the union agree.

If you and the union cannot reach agreement on access, the CAC will determine what access arrangements should be put in place. They must do this within 10 working days.

For more information on how the CAC makes its decision, read the [Code of Practice on access and unfair practices during the recognition and derecognition process](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/code-of-practice-access-and-unfair-practices-during-recognition-and-derecognition-ballots).

## Making an access agreement

When making an access agreement, you should consider:

- physical access to workplaces
- digital access, for example virtual meetings
- written access, for example distribution of written materials

During the access period, you and the union should try to meet:

- once every 5 working days
- for a minimum of 45 minutes

You and the union should implement access arrangements flexibly.

The access period starts once an access agreement has been agreed or determined by the CAC and runs until the ballot on union recognition closes.

## Employer duties

You must comply with the access agreement once it’s in place. If you do not, the union can complain to the CAC. If the CAC finds you have breached the agreement, they may:

- tell you to take reasonable steps to comply with it
- automatically recognise the union if you do not

You also have the right to complain to the CAC if the union is not complying with the access agreement.

## Ballot on union recognition

Your workers will be balloted about whether they want the union to be recognised if the majority of workers in the bargaining unit are not members of the union.

The Central Arbitration Committee (CAC) may hold a ballot even if the majority of your workers are union members but they believe any of the following:

- it’ll help maintain good relations between you and your workers
- there’s evidence that a significant number of union members in the bargaining unit do not want the union to represent them
- there are concerns about why some members joined the union, for example, they were pressured

The CAC will ask for your views and the union’s on these issues.

## Before the ballot

You’ll get a letter telling you whether there’ll be a ballot. A ballot can be held at the workplace, by post or both. The CAC will ask for your views and the union’s before they decide what kind of ballot to hold.

The union has 10 days to withdraw its application. If it does not, the CAC will appoint a qualified independent person (QIP) to run the ballot.

The ballot will usually be held within 20 working days of the QIP being appointed.

### Your responsibilities

The cost of the ballot is split equally between you and the union.

You must:

- make sure the union can talk to workers in the bargaining unit - for example, by allowing them to hold meetings without you
- not ask workers to miss meetings, unless it’s absolutely necessary
- not threaten or take any action against a worker because they attended a meeting

The union can [complain to the CAC](https://www.gov.uk/complain-about-unfair-practice-during-a-recognition-ballot) if they think you have not co-operated.

## After the ballot

You’ll usually find out the result of the vote about a week after the ballot closes.

The CAC will declare the union is recognised if the majority of workers in the ballot vote to recognise the union.

### If the CAC declares the union is recognised

You must [work with the union](https://www.gov.uk/working-with-trade-unions) and work out how to collectively bargain on:

- pay
- hours
- holiday entitlement

### If the CAC does not declare the union is recognised

The union will not be able to apply for statutory recognition for 3 years, but you can still recognise them voluntarily.

## Complaints about and from the union

You can complain to the Central Arbitration Committee (CAC) if the union tries to influence the outcome of a ballot using ‘unfair practices’, for example, by trying to pressurise workers.

The [code of practice on access and unfair practices during the recognition and derecognition process](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/code-of-practice-access-and-unfair-practices-during-the-recognition-and-derecognition-process) has more information.

Make your complaint in writing to the CAC. You can complain at any time until the day after the ballot closes.

## What happens next

The CAC may:

- restart the ballot process
- give the union a final warning, sometimes called a ‘remedial order’

If the union does not do what the order tells it to do, the CAC can cancel the ballot and declare the union is not recognised

## If the union complains

The union can complain if you:

- do not co-operate with preparations for the ballot
- use [unfair practices](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/code-of-practice-access-and-unfair-practices-during-recognition-and-derecognition-ballots) during the recognition process, including to change the outcome of the ballot

The CAC may give you a final warning if the union’s complaint is successful, sometimes called a ‘remedial order’.

The CAC can also:

- restart the ballot process
- cancel the ballot and declare the union is recognised

## Ending negotiations

You and the union will not need to continue negotiations if the union withdraws its application. They must do this before the Central Arbitration Committee (CAC) either :

- declares them recognised or not recognised
- tells you that they’re holding a ballot of your workers

## You’re recognising the union voluntarily

If the union is withdrawing its application because you’ve agreed to recognise them, you and the union can tell the CAC by sending a ‘joint notice to cease consideration’. This means the CAC will no longer be involved.

Send the CAC a letter signed by both you and the representative of the union.

You must do this before the CAC declares the union is recognised or up to the last day of the ballot notification period (10 working days from when the CAC told you about the ballot).
