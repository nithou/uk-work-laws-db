# Jobcentre Plus help for recruiters

_Source: GOV.UK — https://www.gov.uk/jobcentre-plus-help-for-recruiters — updated 2015-07-10_

## Overview

Jobcentre Plus has a range of recruitment services that can help you as an employer. You could get:

- [recruitment advice](https://www.gov.uk/jobcentre-plus-help-for-recruiters/recruitment-advice-and-support), including support with your vacancies
- help setting up [work trials](https://www.gov.uk/jobcentre-plus-help-for-recruiters/work-trials) to give you the opportunity to try out potential recruits
- advice about offering [work experience and apprenticeships](https://www.gov.uk/jobcentre-plus-help-for-recruiters/work-experience-apprenticeships), including using the sector-based work academy programme (SWAP)
- support if you [employ someone with a disability](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-guide-for-employers) (Access to Work)
- advice and guidance on [employing someone with a disability or health condition](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/disability-confident-campaign)

You can also advertise a job for free with the [‘Find a job’ service](https://www.gov.uk/advertise-job).

This guide is also available [in Welsh (Cymraeg)](https://www.gov.uk/cymorth-i-recriwtwyr-gan-y-ganolfan-byd-gwaith).

## Recruitment advice and support

Contact the Employer Services Line for advice about recruiting for your business.

It will connect you to a local recruitment consultant who will work with you to fill your vacancies. Recruitment consultants can:

- give advice to help you write job descriptions
- help you speed up your recruitment process
- promote your vacancies in local job centres and on the Jobcentre Plus social media accounts
- allow you to use Jobcentre Plus offices to carry out interviews, where an office is available
- invite you to local recruitment events to promote your vacancies, for example jobs fairs
- help you connect with other businesses in your area
- work with you to plan your future recruitment campaigns
- help to match the right candidate to your vacancies, including sifting applications and interviewing candidates for you

- you’re a large employer (with over 250 employees)
- you’re expanding
- you’re opening a new business site
- you’re recruiting across a number of locations

## Work trials

A work trial is a short period in work you can offer to a jobseeker on benefits. It’s a way for you both to see if the job is a good fit.

It happens after you’ve interviewed them for a specific role. If they’re not suitable for it, you do not need to offer it to them.

Jobseekers volunteer for a work trial. They keep getting their benefits whilst they’re on it and are not paid a wage.

## Eligibility

The work trial must:

- only be used as a way for you and the potential employee to decide if they’re right for the role
- be for a job where the jobseeker is the only person you’re considering hiring

You need to agree the length of the work trial with the jobseeker before it starts. It must:

- end when you’re sure about whether the jobseeker is suitable for the role
- last no more than 5 days if the job is for less than 6 months
- last no more than 30 days (and usually around 5 days) for jobs lasting 6 months or more

Jobcentre Plus will check that the employee has volunteered for the trial and that it meets the eligibility criteria.

## How to carry out a work trial

You need to agree a work trial with Jobcentre Plus before you offer it to a jobseeker.

[Contact the Employer Services Line](https://www.gov.uk/jobcentre-plus-help-for-recruiters/recruitment-advice-and-support) to find out more.

## Work experience and apprenticeships

## Work experience

Work experience is available to:

- all 16 to 24 year olds
- people aged 25 and over who do not have any recent work history

If you offer a young person work experience you’ll be helping to give them a better chance of finding work.

Read the [work experience guidance for employers](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/employers-could-you-offer-work-experience) to find out what’s expected of you.

[Contact the Employer Services Line](https://www.gov.uk/jobcentre-plus-help-for-recruiters/recruitment-advice-and-support) if you want help to become a work experience host.

## Apprenticeships

These are organised through the Apprenticeship service and often follow a period of work experience. They combine practical training with study.

If you [take on an apprentice](https://www.gov.uk/take-on-an-apprentice), you can get funding to train them. You might also be able to get an [apprenticeship grant](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/apprenticeship-grant-for-employers-of-16-to-24-year-olds) if you run a small or medium-sized business.

Apprenticeships are different in [Scotland](https://www.apprenticeships.scot/), [Wales](https://businesswales.gov.wales/skillsgateway/apprenticeships) and [Northern Ireland](https://www.nibusinessinfo.co.uk/content/apprenticeships-delivering-work-based-training-and-qualifications-employees).

## Sector-based work academy programme

The [sector-based work academy programme](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/sector-based-work-academies-employer-guide) (SWAP) is a Jobcentre Plus employment scheme. It can help you fill your vacancies more effectively by providing training, work experience and a guaranteed job interview.

The sector-based work academy programme is only available in England and Scotland.
