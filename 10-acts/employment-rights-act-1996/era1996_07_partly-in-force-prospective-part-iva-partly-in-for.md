### Section 43B — ⟦partly in force / prospective: Disclosures qualifying for protection.⟧
[legislation.gov.uk](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1996/18/section/43B) · extent: E+W+S

(1) ⟦partly in force / prospective: In this Part a “⟧⟦partly in force / prospective: qualifying disclosure⟧⟦partly in force / prospective: ” means any disclosure of information which, in the reasonable belief of the worker making the disclosure,⟧⟦partly in force / prospective: is made in the public interest and⟧⟦partly in force / prospective: tends to show one or more of the following—⟧
  (a) ⟦partly in force / prospective: that a criminal offence has been committed, is being committed or is likely to be committed,⟧
  (b) ⟦partly in force / prospective: that a person has failed, is failing or is likely to fail to comply with any legal obligation to which he is subject,⟧
  (c) ⟦partly in force / prospective: that a miscarriage of justice has occurred, is occurring or is likely to occur,⟧
  (d) ⟦partly in force / prospective: that the health or safety of any individual has been, is being or is likely to be endangered,⟧
  (da) ⟦partly in force / prospective: that sexual harassment has occurred, is occurring or is likely to occur,⟧
  (e) ⟦partly in force / prospective: that the environment has been, is being or is likely to be damaged, or⟧
  (f) ⟦partly in force / prospective: that information tending to show any matter falling within any one of the preceding paragraphs has been, is being or is likely to be deliberately concealed.⟧
(2) ⟦partly in force / prospective: For the purposes of subsection (1), it is immaterial whether the relevant failure occurred, occurs or would occur in the United Kingdom or elsewhere, and whether the law applying to it is that of the United Kingdom or of any other country or territory.⟧
(3) ⟦partly in force / prospective: A disclosure of information is not a qualifying disclosure if the person making the disclosure commits an offence by making it.⟧
(4) ⟦partly in force / prospective: A disclosure of information in respect of which a claim to legal professional privilege (or, in Scotland, to confidentiality as between client and professional legal adviser) could be maintained in legal proceedings is not a qualifying disclosure if it is made by a person to whom the information had been disclosed in the course of obtaining legal advice.⟧
(5) ⟦partly in force / prospective: In this Part “⟧⟦partly in force / prospective: the relevant failure⟧⟦partly in force / prospective: ”, in relation to a qualifying disclosure, means the matter falling within paragraphs (a) to (f) of subsection (1).⟧
